Last month I published [forestboard](/on-building-a-keyboard), but I wasn't
entirely happy with it. The screen sat too far below the sheet metal
plate, and was so off center that it just didn't quite look right. Due to
an electrical problem with the first board, I knew that I was going to build up
another keyboard anyway, so I decided to take a stab at fixing the screen position.

Objective: center the screen & tilt it up so it's easier to read. Stretch goal? Use **no fasteners**!

I have a history of building things with an unreasonable number of fasteners.
Screws, nuts, washers, captured hardware, etc. For this part, I want a snug fit with no extra hardware. 3D printer PLA is
pliable enough that I should be able to snap everything together. Snap fits
can be tricky--you need strength and motion in small parts, and those are
often in conflict. I noticed that OnShape has a FeatureScript MCP server, so
I figured I'd give it a shot.

## Describe, print, repeat

<Img idx={7} width="xl" rounded="sm" shadow clickForFullscreen alt="Collection of printed screen mount revisions and faceplates" />
<Caption>{"v1 to v5"}</Caption>

I used Sol 6.1 to help generate each iteration of the mount. I generally wrote no more than
one paragraph of prompt text per version, with some accompanying measurements.
 FeatureScript MCP wasn't able to cleanly read existing OnShape
sketch geometry, which was a bit odd. Nonetheless, the dimensions of the screen PCB
were readily available, and I slung it the sheet metal dims, and then it was on its merry way.

## v1

<Img idx={2} width="xl" rounded="sm" shadow clickForFullscreen alt="First printed version of the screen mount" />
<Caption>{"v1 - Quick n Dirty"}</Caption>

The first version is a starting point. Garbage in, garbage out. I really wanted
to focus on the screen PCB dimensions and the amount of horizontal offset.
It was clearly much too big!

<Img idx={3} width="lg" rounded="sm" shadow clickForFullscreen alt="Second version of the mount with clips in the wrong position" />
<Caption>{"V2: clips, but in the wrong place."}</Caption>

I AI slopped the 2nd variant in. This one focused on being lower profile, but the AI ignored the sheet metal dimensions, and I didn't double check it.

<Img idx={4} width="lg" rounded="sm" shadow clickForFullscreen alt="Third version of the mount during alignment checks" />
<Caption>{"v3 - working on alignment"}</Caption>

The third version was actually pretty decent! However, there were not enough
features to firmly locate the mount in place. It rotated somewhat freely, so
I needed to lock it down.

<Img idx={5} width="xl" rounded="sm" shadow clickForFullscreen alt="Fourth and fifth mount versions side by side, with the screen fitted into one" />
<Caption>{"v5 & v4, respectively."}</Caption>

v4 worked fine, but the bezel piece was quite flimsy. You can see me pointing to
the bezel clip on the v5 variant--it's substantially larger. It took almost no force
to pop the bezel off, making it feel cheap and unfinished. The v5 variant is firm
and keeps the screen robustly in place.

<Img idx={6} rounded="sm" shadow clickForFullscreen alt="CAD model of the screen mount and faceplate, showing their clips" />
<Caption>{"v5 in Onshape. Plenty of little features for a little screen."}</Caption>


All in all, I probably invested only 15 minutes total for the actual *design* and CAD
aspects of this project. It's staggeringly low. I spent plenty of time
babysitting the AI, setting up the prints, and troubleshooting fit issues. However,
I've never spent so little time on the actual design work. It may seem like
just two simple parts--but they really are quite complex! It's precision fit geometry
with rigid components and flexible snaps. I'm still floored on how easy it was.
Sadly, OnShape gives you only 2.5k total annual API calls. It's absurdly low,
so I may have to try out FreeCAD MCP or even just openscad.
